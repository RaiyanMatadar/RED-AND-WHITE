# The Switch & Why We Need It

## What is a Switch?

A **switch** is a networking device that connects multiple computers together using cables, forming a local network.

> Think of it as a central hub — instead of connecting every computer directly to each other, they all plug into the switch.

---

## Access Points vs Switches

| Device | Connection Type |
|---|---|
| **Access Point** | Wireless (Wi-Fi) |
| **Switch** | Wired (Cables) |

---

## Why Do We Need a Switch?

Without a switch, connecting multiple computers would be a mess — you'd need a cable between every single pair of machines. A switch solves this by giving every device **one cable to one place**.

It allows you to:
- Connect many computers through a single device
- Share resources (files, printers, internet) across machines
- Build a structured, manageable local network

---

## What is a LAN?

A **LAN (Local Area Network)** is a network that connects computers within a **restricted physical area**, such as:

- A home or residence
- A school or university campus
- A laboratory
- An office building

---

## Packets vs Frames

These two terms refer to the **same concept** — a chunk of data being sent across a network — just used at different layers of networking. For simplicity, we'll use the term **packet** throughout.

---

> **In short:** A switch is what makes a LAN possible. It's the device that ties all your wired devices together into one connected network.
