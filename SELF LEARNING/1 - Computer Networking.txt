---TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)---
When you need accuracy.
Connection-based (handshake happens before data transfer).
Reliable: ensures every packet reaches the destination.
Orders data: packets arrive in correct sequence.
Resends lost packets.
Slower because of reliability checks and confirmations.

Used for:
Web browsing (HTTP/HTTPS)
File download/upload
Emails
Anything that must not lose data

Why TCP exists:
If you’re loading a webpage or downloading a file, a missing packet breaks the whole thing. TCP prevents that.

---UDP (User Datagram Protocol)---
When you need speed.
Connectionless (no handshake, just sends).
Unreliable: packets can be lost, duplicated, or arrive out of order.
No packet ordering.
No resending.
Faster because it skips all checks.

Used for:
Online gaming
Video calls
Livestreams
DNS lookup
Anything where speed > perfection

Why UDP exists:
If you’re in a voice call, losing a few packets is better than having delays. You want real-time, not perfection.
